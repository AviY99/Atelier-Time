## Observed source — what the service really does

- `android-native/app/src/main/AndroidManifest.xml`: `POST_NOTIFICATIONS`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE`, `FOREGROUND_SERVICE_SPECIAL_USE`; `ClassNotificationTaskService` is non-exported, declares `foregroundServiceType="specialUse"` and a service subtype property. The subtype text currently says "Active user-initiated drawing class countdown; remove status when task is dismissed" — the *service* actually displays/maintains status and handles task removal, **not** countdown computation.
- `ClassNotificationTaskService.kt`: launched via `startForegroundService` after a user-started Class when notifications are permitted; calls `startForeground(..., FOREGROUND_SERVICE_TYPE_SPECIAL_USE)` on Android 14+, returns `START_NOT_STICKY`, stops on failure, removes notification in `onTaskRemoved` / `onDestroy`. Does not own timer deadlines, calculate next cues or play sound.
- `ActiveClassStatusNotification.kt`: Compose-owned timer snapshots drive low-importance, silent chronometer notification; optional notification declines do not block Class or sounds. Status is updated during RUNNING/PAUSED/STOPPED views, and cleared on finish/exit; `setTimeoutAfter` is a stale-notification safeguard, not a timer engine.
- `MainActivity.kt`: explicit Back calls `finish()`; finishing calls service cleanup; Home and Recents overview are distinguished from Back/task close. Previous Samsung background, lock, cues and exit cleanup behavior was accepted; do not reopen this QA without a relevant change.
- `SessionEditorScreen.kt` + `MelodyAudio.kt`: timer and sound playback remain managed outside the notification service. Therefore **do not claim** `mediaPlayback` FGS eligibility or claim the service performs indispensable audio/timekeeping.

## External requirements checked (30 October 2026)

1. Android: Android 14+ foreground service must declare matching type and type-specific permission. `specialUse` has no additional runtime prerequisites but requires a manifest subtype explanation; actual uses are reviewed in Play Console. **Manifest structure is consistent** — this is not a Play eligibility approval. Official: https://developer.android.com/develop/background-work/services/fgs/service-types
2. Google Play: any foreground-service permission use must benefit a core function, be user-started/perceptible, user-stoppable, limited to necessary duration, **and cannot be safely interrupted/deferred** without loss of intended feature/user experience. The service's optional status/helper function makes that final criterion **not yet demonstrated**, although a live status and non-stale cleanup are meaningful user benefits. Official: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16559646
3. Play Console for apps targeting Android 14+: each FGS type needs a truthful feature description, explicit consequences of deferral and interruption, a video demonstrating user-triggered behavior, and an appropriate use case. Official: https://support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/13392821
